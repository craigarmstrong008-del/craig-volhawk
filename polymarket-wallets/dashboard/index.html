    How this is built

    1. Candidates. Polymarket's leaderboard has no 90-day window, so wallets are pulled from four boards (30-day PnL, 30-day volume, all-time PnL, 7-day PnL) and merged.
    2. History. For each wallet the tool downloads every on-chain action in the window (trades, redemptions, splits, merges, rewards), every position closed in the window, its open positions and its portfolio value. Everything comes from data-api.polymarket.com.
    3. 90-day PnL comes from Polymarket's own profit history for each wallet (the data behind the chart on its profile page): today's figure minus the figure 90 days ago. If that history isn't available, the tool falls back to a position-level estimate, shown separately in each wallet's panel.
    4. Realised PnL is the sum of realizedPnl on positions closed inside the window, plus resolved positions that were never redeemed (mostly losing bets, which pay $0 so traders often leave them). Unrealised is the mark-to-market of open, unresolved positions. Some open positions may have been opened before the window started, so 90-day PnL (realised + unrealised) is an estimate.
    5. Win rate counts closed positions with positive vs. negative realised PnL. Profit factor = gross wins ÷ gross losses. ROI = realised PnL ÷ cost basis of closed positions. Sharpe is annualised from daily realised PnL.
    6. Score (0–100) is a weighted mix of percentile ranks within this cohort: 90-day PnL 25%, realised PnL 20%, win rate (shrunk toward 50%) 15%, profit factor 15%, ROI 15%, Sharpe 10%. Wallets with fewer than 15 closed positions are pulled toward 50 to account for luck.
    7. Categories come from keyword rules on market titles, so they're approximate. Archetypes are rule-based tags (for example, "Favourite grinder" means at least 60% of buy dollars went in at 80¢ or more).

    This is not financial advice. Past performance on prediction markets is noisy, and copy-trading lags the original fill.
